Owner: Kamen Kamenov, 2026-09-05. Claude inspects after the BUILD-LEDGER entry.

## Frozen defects

Claude's Atom 14 inspection found two blockers. The native approval window names only the prose
field and repository, so an operator cannot see which atom and exact request receives the choice.
The installed controller starts a United Partners atom but both validation and final authorization
refuse because blocker-closeout support exists only in the canonical memory-knowledge repository.

The same inspection records four trust notes: helper rebuilds can change Keychain access identity;
approving an untrusted `security` process would expose the key; shell-launched parent identity was
recorded as `unknown`; and Atom 14's blocker-ledger evidence was not published with its build.

## Approved atomic outcome

The native window and macOS authentication reason visibly name the atom id and full request hash.
Those identities are signed and checked at `start`. A newly produced receipt cannot cross atoms or
requests. The one real version-1 Atom 14 receipt remains valid only for its exact recorded request.

The managed installer records the canonical support repository and exact hashes of the two modules
that own blocker closeout. Either installed projection can then complete `record-validation` and
`authorize-next` for an atom in another repository through that recorded source. Missing, changed,
linked, or incomplete provenance refuses with an actionable managed-install message.

Parent-process telemetry reports the executable path/name actually observed, or an explicit
unavailable state. Operator guidance states that a rebuilt helper can require a new Keychain access
confirmation and that access must never be granted to `security`, a shell, or another untrusted
reader. Atom 15's ledger and eventual publish scope include the omitted blocker evidence needed to
make its closeout reproducible.
