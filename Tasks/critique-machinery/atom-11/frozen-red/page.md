# Tactical Roadmap

The phased twelve-month sequence of the approved platform: signature moments balanced against an always-on presence, every tactic an Activation Card placed by date, channel, audience, message, phase and KPI (key performance indicator).

**The platform is approved: The Stop Line, decided by Kamen Kamenov.**
**The owners and the calendar below are not yet approved; no owner is recorded.**

**Period and markets.** The campaign period runs for twelve months up to 30 June 2027 across the United States, the European Union and Sofia.

## The phases

| phase | objective | months |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Seed | Prepare the proof base, approval route and owner-message evidence before The Stop Line is promoted. | 1-3 |
| Launch | Introduce The Stop Line through Signed by Humans and direct priority audiences to approved proof. | 4-5 |
| Sustain | Keep the platform visible through proof, explanation, partner enablement and senior craft. | 6-9 |
| Amplify | Use proof, third-party credibility and trained advocates to widen the story without widening the claim. | 10-12 |

The four phases are the year. Inside a phase, each major moment runs Tease → Launch → Sustain → Amplify; across the year the corporate rollout widens the audience Engage → Enhance → Advocate → Amplify.

## The Hero / Hub / Hygiene balance

**Hero — signature moments, about a fifth.** Hero carries about a fifth of the effort through The Stop Line proof page, the Month 4 launch, Signed by Humans founder launch and earned validation outreach.

**Hub — sustained programmes, about three fifths.** Hub carries about three fifths of the effort through message testing, fact sheets, partner proof, technical proof, social activation, senior craft stories and advocacy enablement.

**Hygiene — always-on maintenance, about a fifth.** Hygiene carries about a fifth of the effort through public identity cleanup, proof-library governance, message-table control, spokesperson readiness, conversation management and the close scorecard.

## The Activation Cards

No tactic goes on the calendar until it is a card.

### Public Identity Cleanup

**Insight.** A proof-led AI story fails if buyer-facing records conflict before the proof is seen.

**Idea.** Clean The B Team's public identity around The Stop Line before launch so the market sees one consistent proof-first story; any cleaned public record is live, unpromoted, until the Launch moment in Month 4.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Buyers, partners and media encounter consistent public records before being asked to evaluate The Stop Line.

**Desired outcome.** The launch has no unresolved public-profile conflicts undermining credibility.  ·  **Targets.** Public website, LinkedIn profiles, sales decks, media boilerplate and buyer-facing descriptions read in Month 1; conflicts logged and resolved before Month 4.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Public-profile consistency rate measured as percentage of identified buyer-facing records passing the Public Identity Cleanup Checklist; unresolved conflict count; approved boilerplate adoption rate.  ·  **Month.** 1

### Proof Library Lock

**Insight.** The Stop Line can only be ownable if every AI claim shows approved proof, a senior owner and a stated limit.

**Idea.** Lock the proof library behind The Stop Line before promotion, with every claim labelled as named reference, verifiable anonymous reference, technical artifact or internal demo.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Internal reviewers read each claim against its proof type, owner and stop-line limit before it can enter public use.

**Desired outcome.** The year starts from an approved proof base instead of a list of AI claims.  ·  **Targets.** All public AI claims, proof labels, senior-owner fields and stated limits read in Month 1; launch claims approved before Month 4.

**Signature.** Proof before promotion  ·  **KPI.** Percentage of launch AI claims linked to approved proof; percentage of proof entries passing the Proof Library Lock Checklist; number of claims rejected or narrowed before publication.  ·  **Month.** 1

### Key-Message Table

**Insight.** A distributed launch needs one approved source of message truth or local teams will stretch the claim.

**Idea.** Produce the Key-Message Table for The Stop Line and Signed by Humans as the approved training source for audiences, proof points and do-not-say lines.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Spokespeople, sales and partners read one approved message table before creating launch or follow-up material.

**Desired outcome.** Every tactic uses the same proof-first restraint language.  ·  **Targets.** Audience messages, proof points, do-not-say lines and approval owners read in Month 1; table used from Month 1 through Month 12.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Message-table completion rate; percentage of assets passing the Key-Message Table Alignment Rubric on audience fit, proof trace, claim limit and do-not-say compliance; approved-answer reuse rate.  ·  **Month.** 1

### The Stop Line Proof Page

**Insight.** Buyers do not need a bigger AI promise; they need to see what is proved, who owns it and where the system stops.

**Idea.** Create one owned page for The Stop Line that is live, unpromoted, until the Launch moment in Month 4, showing each approved claim with proof type, senior owner and stated limit.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Visitors can inspect the proof behind the claim before they are asked to book a meeting or accept the AI story.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team has a public proof base that paid, earned, shared and sales activity can all point to.  ·  **Targets.** Proof page read internally in Month 2 and published live, unpromoted, from Month 2 until launch promotion in Month 4; all launch claims checked before Month 4.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Proof-page visits; time on page; qualified meeting clicks; percentage of public AI claims linked to approved proof; page quality scored on a 1-5 Stop Line Proof Page Checklist covering proof type, senior owner, limit and buyer clarity.  ·  **Month.** 2

### Fact Sheet and Backgrounder

**Insight.** Media, partners and technical buyers need the approval route and claim limits in a form they can cite safely.

**Idea.** Build the fact sheet and backgrounder explaining The Stop Line, Signed by Humans, proof types, senior sign-off and claim boundaries.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Readers get a compact reference that answers what is proved, who approved it and what is not being claimed.

**Desired outcome.** Earned, partner and sales conversations stay inside the approved proof boundary.  ·  **Targets.** Fact sheet and backgrounder read in Month 2; used from Month 4 through Month 12 for launch, partner and earned activity.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** Approval-route accuracy rate; percentage of backgrounder sections passing the Fact Sheet Accuracy Checklist; proof citations used in briefings; media-question answer reuse.  ·  **Month.** 2

### Owner Control Message Test

**Insight.** Owners may value control and visibility more than AI capability, but that remains a hypothesis until owners compare the messages.

**Idea.** Test a control-led message against an AI-capability message, both anchored in The Stop Line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Owners react to plain-language options showing visibility, ownership and early risk detection without being asked to become technical buyers.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team learns whether control should lead owner-facing communication before scaling the message.  ·  **Targets.** Owner test responses read in Month 3; preference, comprehension, trust and meeting-intent evidence reviewed before Month 4 launch language is promoted.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Preference split; comprehension score on a 1-5 owner-clarity scale; trust lift; meeting intent; qualitative reasons coded against the Owner Control Message Test Rubric.  ·  **Month.** 3

### Spokesperson Training and Q&A

**Insight.** The Stop Line only holds if spokespeople can narrow a claim when proof is missing instead of defending a broad AI promise.

**Idea.** Train founders and senior leaders using the Key-Message Table, 30-second pitch and Q&A before anything is promoted.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Spokespeople rehearse buyer, partner and media questions and learn the approved escalation route.

**Desired outcome.** Launch answers stay consistent, specific and proof-led.  ·  **Targets.** Spokesperson materials, Q&A and 30-second pitch read in Month 3; every launch spokesperson trained before Month 4.

**Signature.** Proof before promotion  ·  **KPI.** Training completion rate; answer quality scored on a 1-5 Spokesperson Stop Line Rubric for proof trace, owner naming, limit clarity and escalation discipline; number of unapproved claims corrected before launch.  ·  **Month.** 3

### The Stop Line Launch

**Insight.** The reputation gap is credibility, not reach, so the launch must point to proof before promotion.

**Idea.** Publicly launch The Stop Line with the proof page, press release and approved fact sheet.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Audiences are directed from launch coverage and owned channels into the proof page where claims can be inspected.

**Desired outcome.** The market sees The B Team's AI story as governed, checked and reviewable.  ·  **Targets.** Launch assets published in Month 4; proof page, press release, fact sheet and approved claims read by owned, earned and sales audiences during the launch window.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Proof-page visits; earned coverage quality scored on a 1-5 Launch Coverage Quality Rubric for proof citation, claim accuracy and stop-line mention; qualified meeting clicks; launch-asset approval pass rate.  ·  **Month.** 4

### Signed by Humans Founder Launch Post

**Insight.** The human moment makes the AI story credible when a senior person visibly checks, owns and signs the work.

**Idea.** Publish a founder-led launch post that explains Signed by Humans as the visible promise behind The Stop Line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Readers see the senior responsibility behind the claim and are directed to the owned proof page for evidence.

**Desired outcome.** Founder visibility increases while the public story stays inside the approved proof boundary.  ·  **Targets.** Founder launch post published in Month 5; reactions, proof-page clicks, comments and partner reshares read during Month 5 before paid amplification is scaled.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** Founder-post reach; engagement quality scored on a 1-5 Signed by Humans Engagement Rubric for relevance, proof-seeking, partner value and talent signal; proof-page clicks; partner reshares; qualified inbound conversations.  ·  **Month.** 5

### Social Pack Activation

**Insight.** The platform needs repeatable shared language so executives, team members and partners do not improvise claims.

**Idea.** Release an approved social pack for The Stop Line and Signed by Humans after the founder launch post.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Participants select approved posts, proof links and visual assets rather than writing from scratch.

**Desired outcome.** Shared content reinforces the proof base without widening the AI claim.  ·  **Targets.** Social pack published in Month 5 and used from Month 5 through Month 11; posts read weekly during active months for claim compliance.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** Approved-post use rate; claim-compliance rate scored against the Social Pack Compliance Checklist; proof-page clicks; engagement quality scored on a 1-5 Social Pack Engagement Rubric for relevance, proof-seeking and safe-share value; partner and employee participation.  ·  **Month.** 5

### Partner Proof Pack

**Insight.** Referral partners can carry the story only if the approved proof is simple, repeatable and safe to share.

**Idea.** Build and brief a short partner pack that explains The Stop Line, Signed by Humans and the approved proof types behind each claim.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Partners get language they can use without inventing claims or stretching evidence.

**Desired outcome.** Referral conversations become easier, more consistent and less exposed to unapproved AI claims.  ·  **Targets.** Partner pack published and briefed in Month 6; partner use read in Months 6, 10 and 11 when partner and earned activity run.

**Signature.** Proof before promotion  ·  **KPI.** Partner pack downloads; partner briefings completed; referred qualified leads; claim-compliance checks scored on a 1-5 Partner Proof Pack Rubric for proof type, repeatability, limit clarity and safe-share language.  ·  **Month.** 6

### Technical Proof Story

**Insight.** Technical buyers need proof that AI supports delivery without taking ownership away from senior people.

**Idea.** Publish approved technical artifacts and an explainer showing how The Stop Line governs AI-supported delivery.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Technical buyers inspect artifacts, approval labels and limits before entering a technical conversation.

**Desired outcome.** The platform gains technical credibility without becoming a broad AI capability claim.  ·  **Targets.** Technical artifact story published in Month 7; technical conversations and artifact use read in Months 7, 10 and 12.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Technical artifact views; time on page; sales use; qualified technical conversations; artifact quality scored on a 1-5 Technical Proof Story Rubric for inspectability, proof label, senior owner and limit clarity.  ·  **Month.** 7

### Senior Craft Story Series

**Insight.** Senior Sofia talent and careful buyers will believe the AI story only if it protects judgment rather than celebrating automation alone.

**Idea.** Publish a recurring monthly series under Signed by Humans that shows how senior people review work, make decisions and define the stop line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Readers see craft, judgment and responsibility inside AI-supported delivery.

**Desired outcome.** The talent story and buyer story reinforce each other without turning The B Team into a broad ethics platform.  ·  **Targets.** Monthly from Month 8 through Month 11; each story read for craft signal, proof link and claim compliance before publication.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** Senior-talent engagement; profile visits; qualified candidate interest; buyer engagement; proof-page clicks; story quality scored on a 1-5 Senior Craft Story Rubric for judgment, responsibility, stop-line clarity and no automation-only framing.  ·  **Month.** 8

### Conversation Management and Observatory Newsroom

**Insight.** Campaign bursts cannot hold a reputation if no one is listening, triaging and responding between moments.

**Idea.** Run the always-on Conversation Management loop and Observatory newsroom around The Stop Line from Month 1, with a visible calendar focus in Month 9.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** The team listens, triages, responds and escalates only when there is approved proof and a clear stop line to show.

**Desired outcome.** Issues, questions and market moments are handled without widening the claim.  ·  **Targets.** Daily from Month 1 through Month 12, with Month 9 as the named calendar focus; AI trust, governance, buyer-risk and proof questions read every working day.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Response time; issue resolution rate; message consistency scored on a 1-5 Conversation Management Checklist; approved-answer reuse; escalation accuracy for crisis tripwires.  ·  **Month.** 9

### Earned Validation Outreach

**Insight.** Third-party credibility makes The Stop Line harder to dismiss after the proof base exists.

**Idea.** Pitch journalists, analysts and named experts only after The Stop Line proof page, fact sheet and partner evidence are ready.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Professional influencers receive proof citations, approved limits and named responsibility rather than a broad AI claim.

**Desired outcome.** The platform gains independent validation without outrunning evidence.  ·  **Targets.** Earned outreach runs in Month 10; briefings, coverage questions and proof citations read weekly during Month 10.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Briefings secured; coverage quality scored on a 1-5 Earned Validation Rubric for independent proof citation, claim accuracy, senior responsibility and stated limits; qualified inbound conversations; proof citations.  ·  **Month.** 10

### Advocacy Enablement Push

**Insight.** Advocacy works only after people have been engaged, trained and given a safe way to repeat the story.

**Idea.** Equip the advocates trained by the Conversation Management loop: employees, senior spokespeople, partners and alumni receive approved advocacy prompts, proof links and escalation rules for Signed by Humans.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Advocates share approved proof-led content and route questions back to the newsroom instead of improvising claims.

**Desired outcome.** The story widens through credible human carriers without leaving the approval boundary.  ·  **Targets.** Advocacy push runs in Month 11; employee, partner and alumni posts read during Month 11 for proof link, claim compliance and question escalation.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** Advocate participation rate; post quality scored on a 1-5 Advocacy Enablement Post Rubric for proof link, human responsibility, stop-line clarity and required disclosure; engagement quality scored on a 1-5 Advocacy Enablement Engagement Rubric for relevance, proof-seeking, safe-share value and question-routing discipline; proof-page clicks measured as UTM (Urchin Tracking Module tracking parameters)-tagged clicks from approved advocacy posts.  ·  **Month.** 11

### Close Scorecard and Message Recalibration

**Insight.** The next year should be built from measured proof, not from untested confidence.

**Idea.** Close the executive scorecard for The Stop Line and recalibrate messages, proof gaps and local targets for the next cycle.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Leadership reviews proof credibility, trust, public-profile consistency, founder visibility, talent pull and qualified-lead evidence before changing the message.

**Desired outcome.** The platform enters the next year with approved changes and named proof gaps.  ·  **Targets.** Scorecard completed in Month 12; campaign data from Months 1 through 12 read before next-year recommendations are approved.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Executive scorecard completion; message changes approved; next-year proof gaps named; KPI quality scored on a 1-5 Scorecard Recalibration Rubric for evidence source, audience trace, decision usefulness and claim-boundary discipline.  ·  **Month.** 12

## The always-on loop

Underneath the phases runs the Conversation Management loop — supported by the always-on newsroom running proactive stories and reactive issues management: establish goals → gather assets → develop proprietary content → deploy across owned, earned and paid → train advocates → deploy the trained advocates → measure → refine. The advocates are employees, senior spokespeople, referral partners, respected alumni, fans and followers, equipped by Advocacy Enablement Push in Month 11.

## The proof-building order

We build proof in the order the strategy can stand behind: first, the senior owner approves the case base and proof library in writing, so every The Stop Line claim has a proof type, senior owner and stated limit; then thought leadership explains Signed by Humans through founder posts, technical artifacts, buyer explainers and senior craft stories; only after that do we widen visibility through partner briefing, earned validation, paid amplification and advocacy.

## The rollout

**First audience.** The year speaks first to owners of midsize businesses, especially Elena, who wants control and visibility without becoming a technical buyer.

**Then widen.** After that audience has been tested and the proof base is live, the story widens to technical buyers, referral partners, ecosystem partners, enterprise buyers, professional influencers, employees and senior Sofia talent using the same The Stop Line proof boundary.
