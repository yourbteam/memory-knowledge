# Tactical Roadmap

The phased twelve-month sequence of the approved platform: signature moments balanced against an always-on presence, every tactic an Activation Card placed by date, channel, audience, message, phase and KPI (key performance indicator).

**The platform is approved: The Stop Line, decided by Kamen Kamenov.**
**The owners and the calendar below are not yet approved; no owner is recorded.**

**Period and markets.** The campaign period is Month 1 through Month 12, mapped to July 2026 through June 2027 and ending no later than 30 June 2027, across the United States, the European Union and Sofia.

## The phases

| phase | objective | months |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-Seed | Prepare The Stop Line proof base, approval route and message evidence before the market sees the artificial intelligence story. | 1 · 2 · 3 |
| Launch | Introduce The Stop Line through Signed by Humans and direct owners, technical buyers, partners and talent to approved proof. | 4 · 5 |
| Sustain | Keep The Stop Line visible through proof, explanation, partner enablement, senior craft and always-on conversation management. | 6 · 7 · 8 · 9 |
| Amplify | Use earned validation, advocates and scorecard learning to widen The Stop Line without widening the claim. | 10 · 11 · 12 |

The four phases are the year. Inside a phase, each major moment runs Tease → Launch → Sustain → Amplify; across the year the corporate rollout widens the audience Engage → Enhance → Advocate → Amplify.

## The Hero / Hub / Hygiene balance

**Hero — signature moments, about a fifth.** Hero effort is about a fifth of the year and carries the signature moments: the Month 4 Stop Line proof launch, the Month 5 Signed by Humans founder launch post and the Month 10 earned validation push.

**Hub — sustained programmes, about three fifths.** Hub effort is about three fifths of the year and carries the sustained programmes: the proof page, owner message test, partner proof pack, technical proof story, senior craft series and advocacy kit.

**Hygiene — always-on maintenance, about a fifth.** Hygiene effort is about a fifth of the year and keeps the platform credible through public identity cleanup, spokesperson readiness, the Conversation Management loop, the Observatory newsroom and the Month 12 scorecard.

## The Activation Cards

No tactic goes on the calendar until it is a card.

### Public identity cleanup and claim register

**Insight.** The Stop Line cannot build trust if public records carry conflicts or claims that do not point to proof.

**Idea.** Clean buyer-facing public identity and create the claim register that records each AI claim, its proof type, senior owner and stated stop line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Internal reviewers inspect buyer-facing records before any claim is promoted and mark conflicts for closure.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team enters launch with a clean public base and a visible proof-before-promotion route.  ·  **Targets.** Month 1: Kamen Kamenov, Matthew Maday and the assigned public-identity owner read the claim register, public-profile conflict list and Key-Message Table before any Month 2 build work starts.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** public-profile conflicts closed — count, public identity cleanup register · AI claims carrying proof owner and stop-line fields — percentage, The Stop Line claim register  ·  **Month.** 1

### The Stop Line proof page

**Insight.** Buyers do not need a bigger AI promise; they need to see what is proved, who owns it and where the system stops.

**Idea.** Create the owned proof page for The Stop Line, live, unpromoted, until the Launch moment in Month 4, showing each approved claim with proof type, senior owner and stated limit.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Visitors will be able to inspect proof behind a claim before they are asked to book a meeting or accept the AI story.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team has the proof base that paid, earned, shared and sales activity can all point to.  ·  **Targets.** Month 2: Kamen Kamenov, Matthew Maday, sales reviewers and selected partner reviewers read the unpromoted proof page and proof labels before Month 4 promotion.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** approved proof labels completed — percentage, proof-page approval checklist · reviewer confidence in proof clarity — 1-5 score, pre-launch proof-page review rubric  ·  **Month.** 2

### Spokesperson FAQ and 30-second pitch rehearsal

**Insight.** The Stop Line holds only if spokespeople can explain the promise without widening the claim.

**Idea.** Prepare the Q&A (questions and answers), fact sheet and 30-second pitch for spokesperson use, live, unpromoted, until the Launch moment in Month 4, so every spokesperson explains Signed by Humans, The Stop Line and proof before promotion consistently before launch.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Spokespeople rehearse against buyer, partner and media questions and route uncertain claims back to the approval path.

**Desired outcome.** Launch messages stay inside approved proof and do-not-say boundaries.  ·  **Targets.** Month 2: spokespeople read the FAQ, fact sheet and 30-second pitch before the Month 4 proof launch and repeat the approved answer route during rehearsal.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** spokesperson answers staying inside the approved claim boundary — percentage, spokesperson rehearsal checklist · unanswered or escalated claim questions — count, FAQ gap register  ·  **Month.** 2

### Owner control message test

**Insight.** Elena's preference for control and visibility is a strong hypothesis, but it is not settled buyer truth until owners compare the messages.

**Idea.** Test a control-led message against an artificial-intelligence-capability message, both anchored in The Stop Line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Owners react to plain-language options that show visibility, ownership and early risk detection without asking them to become technical buyers.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team learns whether control should lead owner-facing communication before scaling the message.  ·  **Targets.** In Month 3, owners of midsize businesses read both message routes and the unpromoted Stop Line proof explanation before they approve the Launch language in writing for use.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** control-led message preference — percentage, owner message-test report · plain-language comprehension — 1-5 score, owner comprehension rubric · meeting intent after reading the proof route — percentage, owner message-test response form  ·  **Month.** 3

### Conversation Management loop and Observatory newsroom

**Insight.** A platform of proof cannot survive as bursts only; The Stop Line needs daily listening and a clear escalation route.

**Idea.** Run the Conversation Management loop and Observatory newsroom from Month 1 through Month 12 to listen, triage, respond, escalate and identify moments worth entering.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** The team reads live buyer, partner, media and talent signals and uses approved answers only where proof and a stop line exist.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team holds message consistency between campaign moments and catches issues before they widen.  ·  **Targets.** Daily from Month 1 through Month 12: the assigned newsroom team reads the conversation log, issue register and Observatory scan before responding or escalating.

**Signature.** Proof before promotion  ·  **KPI.** responses using approved answer language — percentage, Conversation Management response log · issues escalated before public reply — count, Observatory escalation register · message consistency across live replies — 1-5 score, weekly conversation-quality rubric  ·  **Month.** 1

### The Stop Line proof launch

**Insight.** The public story becomes credible only when the proof page is promoted after approval, not before.

**Idea.** Launch The Stop Line proof page with the press release, proof-page updates and launch-ready owned assets.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Audiences move from the launch announcement into the owned proof page where claims can be inspected.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team gives the market one public proof base for The Stop Line.  ·  **Targets.** Month 4: owners, technical buyers, partners and media read the launch announcement, proof page and press release after Kamen Kamenov and Matthew Maday approvals.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** launch visitors who inspect proof details — percentage, proof-page launch analytics report · qualified meeting clicks from launch assets — count, launch conversion report · earned mentions carrying the proof-before-promotion frame — count, earned coverage review sheet  ·  **Month.** 4

### Signed by Humans founder launch post

**Insight.** The human moment makes the AI story credible when a senior person visibly checks, owns and signs the work.

**Idea.** Publish a founder-led launch post that explains Signed by Humans as the visible promise behind The Stop Line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Readers see the senior responsibility behind the claim and are directed to the owned proof page for evidence.

**Desired outcome.** Founder visibility increases while the public story stays inside the approved proof boundary.  ·  **Targets.** Month 5: owners, technical buyers, referral partners and senior Sofia talent read the founder post and the linked Stop Line proof page after the Month 4 proof launch.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** proof-page clicks from founder content — count, founder-post analytics report · comment and reply quality around senior responsibility — 1-5 score, shared-content engagement-quality rubric · partner reshares preserving approved language — percentage, partner reshare compliance check  ·  **Month.** 5

### Partner proof pack

**Insight.** Referral partners can carry the story only if the approved proof is simple, repeatable and safe to share.

**Idea.** Build a short partner pack that explains The Stop Line, Signed by Humans and the approved proof types behind each claim.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Partners get language they can use without inventing claims or stretching evidence.

**Desired outcome.** Referral conversations become easier, more consistent and less exposed to unapproved AI claims.  ·  **Targets.** Month 6: referral partners, ecosystem partners and enterprise-buyer introducers read the partner proof pack, fact sheet and approved proof examples before briefings.

**Signature.** Proof before promotion  ·  **KPI.** partner briefings completed with approved proof pack — count, partner briefing register · partner introductions using approved language — percentage, partner claim-compliance checklist · referred qualified leads tied to The Stop Line — count, referral lead source report  ·  **Month.** 6

### Technical artifact proof story

**Insight.** Technical buyers need to see that AI supports delivery without taking ownership away from senior people.

**Idea.** Publish a proof story around a technical artifact that shows the claim path from evidence to senior confirmation to the stop line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Technical buyers inspect the artifact and see what is confirmed, what is not claimed and who signs the language before release.

**Desired outcome.** The Stop Line gains technical credibility without becoming a broad AI promise.  ·  **Targets.** Month 7: chief technology officers, architecture owners and sales reviewers read the technical artifact, proof label and claim-limit note before technical conversations.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** technical artifact readers reaching the claim-limit note — percentage, technical proof analytics report · qualified technical conversations started from the artifact — count, technical buyer conversation register · Matthew Maday confirmation checks completed before publication — percentage, technical confirmation checklist  ·  **Month.** 7

### Senior craft story series

**Insight.** Senior Sofia talent and careful buyers will believe the AI story only if it protects judgment rather than celebrating automation alone.

**Idea.** Publish a recurring series under Signed by Humans that shows how senior people review work, make decisions and define the stop line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Readers see craft, judgment and responsibility inside AI-supported delivery.

**Desired outcome.** The talent story and buyer story reinforce each other without turning The B Team into a broad ethics platform.  ·  **Targets.** Monthly from Month 8 through Month 11: senior Sofia talent, buyers and employee advocates read each Signed by Humans craft story and its linked proof note.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** senior-talent readers moving from craft story to proof page — percentage, craft-series analytics report · qualified candidate interest connected to the series — count, talent-interest register · buyer comments recognizing senior judgment — 1-5 score, craft-story engagement-quality rubric  ·  **Month.** 8

### Earned validation outreach

**Insight.** The Stop Line becomes harder to dismiss when credible third parties can inspect the proof and describe the restraint in their own words.

**Idea.** Pitch earned validation only after proof exists, using the fact sheet, proof page and approved claim route.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Journalists, analysts and named experts receive evidence they can check rather than a broad AI claim.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team gains third-party credibility while the public story stays inside approved proof.  ·  **Targets.** Month 10: professional influencers read the fact sheet, proof page, partner proof pack and selected technical artifact before any interview or commentary.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** briefings secured with proof materials read in advance — count, earned outreach briefing register · coverage items citing proof, owner or stop line — count, earned coverage citation review · qualified inbound conversations following earned validation — count, earned-source inbound report  ·  **Month.** 10

### Employee advocacy kit for Conversation Management advocates

**Insight.** Employees and close advocates can repeat The Stop Line only if they have language that keeps proof, senior ownership and limits intact.

**Idea.** Equip the employees, senior delivery leads and partner-facing advocates trained by the Conversation Management loop with approved advocacy prompts, disclosure guidance and proof links.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Advocates choose approved posts or replies, link to proof and escalate uncertain claims rather than improvising.

**Desired outcome.** Advocacy widens visibility without widening the AI claim.  ·  **Targets.** Month 11: employees, senior delivery leads and partner-facing advocates trained by the Conversation Management loop read the advocacy kit, social pack and approved proof links before posting.

**Signature.** Signed by Humans  ·  **KPI.** advocate posts linking to approved proof — percentage, employee advocacy compliance checklist · advocate participation — count, advocacy participation register · post quality against The Stop Line message — 1-5 score, advocacy post-quality rubric  ·  **Month.** 11

### Close scorecard and message recalibration

**Insight.** A proof-led platform needs a disciplined close so the next year improves the proof base instead of repeating unsupported claims.

**Idea.** Close the Month 12 executive scorecard, approve message changes and name next-year proof gaps for The Stop Line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Owners of the programme read the scorecard and decide which proof, message and channel changes are approved for the next cycle.

**Desired outcome.** The Stop Line ends the year with measured learning, approved message changes and named proof gaps.  ·  **Targets.** Month 12: Kamen Kamenov, Matthew Maday and assigned programme owners read the executive scorecard, KPI summary and proof-gap register before next-year planning.

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** scorecard sections completed with evidence — percentage, Month 12 executive scorecard · message changes approved for next cycle — count, Key-Message Table change log · next-year proof gaps named with owner and route — count, proof-gap register  ·  **Month.** 12

## The always-on loop

Underneath the phases runs the Conversation Management loop — supported by the always-on newsroom running proactive stories and reactive issues management: establish goals → gather assets → develop proprietary content → deploy across owned, earned and paid → train advocates → deploy the trained advocates → measure → refine. The advocates are employees, senior delivery leads and partner-facing advocates trained by the Conversation Management loop, equipped by Employee advocacy kit for Conversation Management advocates in Month 11.

## The proof-building order

Cases first: Month 1 Public identity cleanup and claim register, Month 2 The Stop Line proof page, Month 3 Owner control message test and Month 4 The Stop Line proof launch create the evidence base before promotion; thought leadership next: Month 5 Signed by Humans founder launch post, Month 6 Partner proof pack, Month 7 Technical artifact proof story and Month 8 to Month 9 Senior craft story series turn approved proof into repeatable explanation; visibility last: Month 10 Earned validation outreach, Month 10 to Month 11 Senior craft story series amplification, Month 11 Employee advocacy kit for Conversation Management advocates and Month 12 Close scorecard and message recalibration widen the story only after proof and message discipline are in place.

## The rollout

**First audience.** The year speaks first to owners of midsize businesses, represented by Elena, because the control-and-visibility message must be tested before the platform scales.

**Then widen.** After owners have read and tested The Stop Line, the rollout widens to technical buyers, partners, professional influencers, employees, followers and senior Sofia talent through proof pages, technical artifacts, partner packs, earned validation and Signed by Humans advocacy.
