# Tactical Roadmap

The phased twelve-month sequence of the approved platform: signature moments balanced against an always-on presence, every tactic an Activation Card placed by date, channel, audience, message, phase and KPI (key performance indicator).

**The platform is approved: The Stop Line, decided by Kamen Kamenov.**
**The owners and the calendar below are not yet approved; no owner is recorded.**

**Period and markets.** The campaign period is the 12-month approved roadmap year ending no later than 30 June 2027, focused on buyers in the United States and European Union and senior talent in Sofia.

| phase | objective | months |

## The Hero / Hub / Hygiene balance

**Hero — signature moments, about a fifth.** Hero effort is about a fifth of the year and carries the signature moments: The Stop Line proof launch, Signed by Humans founder launch and earned validation outreach.

**Hub — sustained programmes, about three fifths.** Hub effort is about three fifths of the year and carries the sustained programmes: owner message testing, partner proof, technical proof, senior craft and advocacy.

**Hygiene — always-on maintenance, about a fifth.** Hygiene effort is about a fifth of the year and keeps the platform credible through public identity cleanup, daily conversation management, Observatory listening and the close scorecard.

## The Activation Cards

No tactic goes on the calendar until it is a card.

### Public identity and proof lock

**Insight.** The Stop Line is only credible if the public record and proof base are clean before the market sees the AI story.

**Idea.** Clean buyer-facing public identity, lock approved proof types and prepare the Key-Message Table for The Stop Line before promotion.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Internal reviewers inspect every public AI claim against proof type, senior owner and stated limit before any audience-facing asset is released.

**Desired outcome.** The programme starts with a defensible proof base rather than a launch claim that outruns evidence.  ·  **Targets.** Read the public-profile conflict register, proof-library register and Key-Message Table approval log in Month 1 before any launch asset moves forward.  ·  **Approver.** Kamen Kamenov

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Public-profile conflict areas resolved — count, public-profile conflict register · AI claims carrying approved proof labels — percentage, proof-library register · Key-Message Table approval readiness — 1-5 score, pre-publish checklist  ·  **Month.** 1

### Conversation Management loop and Observatory newsroom

**Insight.** The Stop Line cannot hold between campaign moments unless The B Team listens, triages and responds every day.

**Idea.** Run the always-on Conversation Management loop and Observatory newsroom for AI trust, governance and buyer-risk moments from Month 1 through Month 12.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** The team listens, triages, responds and escalates only when an approved proof point and clear stop line can be shown.

**Desired outcome.** The platform stays present and governed between campaign bursts.  ·  **Targets.** Read the daily triage log every working day from Month 1 through Month 12, the weekly response-quality rubric and the monthly Observatory moment register.  ·  **Approver.** Kamen Kamenov

**Signature.** Proof before promotion  ·  **KPI.** Issues answered through approved language — percentage, Conversation Management triage log · Escalations closed with a named senior owner — count, escalation register · Observable moments judged usable for The Stop Line — count, Observatory newsroom register  ·  **Months.** 1 to 12

### The Stop Line proof page

**Insight.** Buyers do not need a bigger AI promise; they need to see what is proved, who owns it and where the system stops.

**Idea.** Create the owned proof page for The Stop Line as a build-and-approval asset, live, unpromoted, until the Launch moment in Month 4.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Visitors can inspect the proof behind each claim before they are asked to book a meeting or accept the AI story.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team has a public proof base that paid, earned, shared and sales activity can all point to.  ·  **Targets.** Read the Month 2 proof-page build checklist, Month 3 approval route check and Month 4 owned analytics report from Month 2 through Month 4.  ·  **Approver.** Kamen Kamenov

**Signature.** The Stop Line  ·  **KPI.** Approved claims displayed with proof type, senior owner and limit — percentage, proof-page checklist · Proof-page inspection depth — 1-5 score, owned analytics review · Qualified meeting clicks from proof-page paths — count, sales-click report  ·  **Months.** 2 to 4

### Owner control message test

**Insight.** Elena's preference for control and visibility is a strong hypothesis, but it is not settled buyer truth until owners compare the messages.

**Idea.** Test a control-led message against an AI-capability message, both anchored in The Stop Line.

**Engagement — the mechanic.** Owners react to plain-language options that show visibility, ownership and early risk detection without asking them to become technical buyers.

**Desired outcome.** The B Team learns whether control should lead owner-facing communication before scaling the message.  ·  **Targets.** Read the Month 3 message-test report before Launch, with the preference split, comprehension notes, trust reading and meeting-intent reasons recorded.  ·  **Approver.** Kamen Kamenov

**Signature.** Owners get control without becoming technical  ·  **KPI.** Control-led message preference — percentage, owner message-test report · Owner comprehension of The Stop Line — 1-5 score, owner message-test rubric · Qualified meeting intent reasons — count, owner response register  ·  **Month.** 3

## The always-on loop

Underneath the phases runs the Conversation Management loop — supported by the always-on newsroom running proactive stories and reactive issues management: establish goals → gather assets → develop proprietary content → deploy across owned, earned and paid → train advocates → deploy the trained advocates → measure → refine. The advocates are employees, senior Sofia advocates and trusted partners trained by the Conversation Management loop, equipped by Employee advocacy push in Month 11; they deploy from Month 11, and until then the loop runs its other steps without them.

## The proof-building order

**Cases first** — Months 1 to 12: Public identity and proof lock, Conversation Management loop and Observatory newsroom, The Stop Line proof page, Owner control message test, Partner proof pack, Technical proof story. This stage proves that The Stop Line has approved evidence, clean public identity, owner-language learning, partner-safe language, technical confirmation and daily proof discipline before visibility is scaled.

**Thought leadership** — Months 5 to 12: Signed by Humans founder launch post, Senior craft story series. This stage proves that the platform can be explained by senior people in public without turning restraint into a broad AI promise.

**Visibility** — Months 10 to 12: Earned validation outreach, Employee advocacy push, Close scorecard and message recalibration. This stage proves that third parties, employees and the executive scorecard can carry The Stop Line while staying inside approved proof and visible limits.

## The rollout

**First audience.** Owners of midsize businesses who want control and visibility without becoming technical buyers.

**Then widen — from Month 5 to Month 12.** After the owner-control reading and proof launch, the roadmap widens to technical buyers, referral partners, professional influencers, employees and senior Sofia talent through Signed by Humans and the approved proof base.
